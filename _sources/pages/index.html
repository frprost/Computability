Foreword

This manual was first written in LaTeX. The format of the printed page imposed a single reading order, whereas the subject lends itself poorly to one: computability interests the programmer who wants to see a Turing machine compute, the mathematician who wants a correct proof, and the curious reader who wants to understand what it means, in everyday life, for a problem to be “undecidable”. The HTML format makes it possible to superimpose these readings instead of juxtaposing them.

The text is self-contained: everything used is defined, either in the chapter or in the appendices. It assumes no knowledge of programming, though it provides a good deal of it, and it assumes nothing more in mathematics than proof by induction.

This English edition is a translation of the French original, Calculabilité, un manuel à trois niveaux; the mathematical notation is the same in both.

How to use this manual: three reading levels

Each result (definition, theorem, proposition) is presented in a box. Its statement is always visible; below it, three tabs give three ways of understanding it:

Philosophical

What does the result mean? Why is it surprising, and what does it imply concretely: for computer science, for science, for everyday life? No formula is indispensable there.

Technical

Mathematical proofs that are correct and complete in their structure, which take for granted only constructions whose realization is routine (“one builds a machine that…”).

Technological

The constructions carried through to the end: complete Turing machine programs, explicit encodings, λ-calculus terms. Most of them run in the page, and their results have been tested automatically.

The selector at the top of each page chooses the level displayed everywhere; the tab of a box changes only that box. The “All” choice displays the three levels one below the other (this is also what gets printed). The choice is remembered by the browser.

The simulators. Four tools are built into the manual and work offline: a Turing machine simulator (in the quadruple format of chapter 3), a compiler that turns a recursive function into a complete Turing machine (often several thousand instructions) and then runs it, a fast busy beaver simulator and a λ-term reducer.

Suggested reading paths

The manual can be read in order, but this is not compulsory. The page Reading paths details each itinerary step by step, together with a training plan based on the exercises.

General notational conventions

This list gathers the notations used in several chapters, with a link to the place where each one is defined. Notations specific to a single proof are introduced on the spot.

Integers, functions, sets

  • $\N=\{0,1,2,\dots\}$; the variables $x,y,z,n,m,a,e$ denote natural numbers unless otherwise stated, and $\vec x$ abbreviates $x_1,\dots,x_n$.
  • “Function” means by default partial function from $\N^n$ to $\N$ (definition). We write $f(x)\conv$ if $f$ is defined at $x$, $f(x)\ind$ otherwise; $\mathrm{dom}\,f$ is the domain of definition.
  • $e_1\simeq e_2$ (Kleene equality): one of the expressions is defined if and only if the other is, and they then have the same value.
  • $x\monus y$ is proper subtraction (monus): $x-y$ if $x\ge y$, $0$ otherwise.
  • $\chi_A$ (sometimes $c_A$) is the characteristic function of $A\subseteq\N$: $1$ on $A$, $0$ elsewhere. $\overline A=\N\setminus A$ is the complement.
  • $x\mapsto e$, or $\lambda x.e$, denotes the function mapping $x$ to $e$ (anonymous notation).
  • $p_1=2,p_2=3,p_3=5,\dots$ are the prime numbers (definition).

Turing machines

  • States $q_0,q_1,\dots$, symbols $S_0,S_1,\dots$, with $B=S_0$ (blank) and $1=S_1$ (stroke). A machine $Z$ is a finite consistent set of quadruples $q_i\,S_j\,S_k\,q_l$, $q_i\,S_j\,R\,q_l$, $q_i\,S_j\,L\,q_l$ (definition); quintuples write and move in a single step.
  • An instantaneous description is a word $\alpha=\gamma\,q_i\,S_j\,\delta$: the tape holds $\gamma S_j\delta$ and the head reads $S_j$; $\alpha\vdash_Z\beta$ is a computation step, $\mathrm{Res}_Z(\alpha)$ the final description if the computation halts (definition).
  • $\overline x=1^{x+1}$ represents the integer $x$; an $n$-tuple is written $\overline{x_1}B\cdots B\overline{x_n}$; $\langle\alpha\rangle$ is the number of strokes in the word $\alpha$ (convention).
  • $\Psi^{(n)}_Z$ is the $n$-argument function computed by $Z$, $\mathcal T$ the class of Turing-computable functions (definition); with an oracle $A$: $Z^A$, $\Psi^{(n)}_{Z^A}$, $\mathcal T^A$ (definition).
  • Radó machines (busy beavers): states $A,B,C,\dots$ and halting state $H$, symbols $0$ (blank) and $1$; $A0\to1RB$ reads “in $A$, reading $0$, write $1$, move right, go to $B$”. $\Sigma(n)$ and $S(n)$ are the largest number of $1$s and the largest number of steps of the halting $n$-state machines (definition).

Recursive functions and encodings

  • Basic functions: constants $m^{(k)}$, successor $S$, projections $A^{(k)}_i$. Schemes: substitution $\sigma[g,h_1,\dots,h_k]$, primitive recursion $\rho[g,h]$, with $\rho[g,h](y+1,\vec x)=h\big(y,\rho[g,h](y,\vec x),\vec x\big)$ (recursion on the first argument); $\RP$ is the class of primitive recursive functions (definition).
  • Minimization: $\mu[g]$, or $\mu y\,[g(\vec x,y)=0]$, the least $y$ at which $g$ vanishes, the previous values being defined; $\mu y\,R(\vec x,y)$ for a predicate $R$; $\mu ydefinition).
  • $\langle x,y\rangle=\frac{(x+y)(x+y+1)}2+x$ is Cantor’s pairing function, with inverses $\pi_1,\pi_2$; $\langle x_1,\dots,x_n\rangle=\langle x_1,\langle x_2,\dots,x_n\rangle\rangle$ (proposition). Context distinguishes this coding from $\langle\alpha\rangle$ (number of strokes in a word).
  • Gödel numbers: $\gamma$ encodes each symbol by an odd integer, $\mathrm{gn}(a_1\cdots a_n)=\prod_kp_k^{\gamma(a_k)}$ encodes a word, and a sequence of words $M_1,\dots,M_n$ is encoded by $\prod_kp_k^{\mathrm{gn}(M_k)}$ (definition). For machines, $\gamma(R)=3$, $\gamma(L)=5$, $\gamma(S_i)=4i+9$, $\gamma(q_i)=4i+7$ (definition).
  • $T_n(z,\vec x,y)$ is Kleene’s predicate and $U(y)$ the result function: $\Psi^{(n)}_Z(\vec x)\simeq U(\mu y\,T_n(z,\vec x,y))$ (normal form); $T^A_n$ with an oracle.

Indices and classical sets

  • $\eta(e)$ is the $e$-th Gödel number of a machine in increasing order; $\varphi^{(k)}_e$ is the $k$-argument function computed by the machine encoded by $\eta(e)$, $\varphi_e=\varphi^{(1)}_e$, and $W_e=\mathrm{dom}\,\varphi_e$ (notation); $\varphi^A_e$, $W^A_e$ with an oracle ($\eta^\ast$ then plays the role of $\eta$). The integer $e$ is an index or program.
  • $s^m_n$ denotes the functions of the s-m-n theorem.
  • $K=\{e:\varphi_e(e)\conv\}$ (diagonal halting), $H=K_0=\{\langle e,x\rangle:\varphi_e(x)\conv\}$ (theorem).
  • $\mathrm{Tot}$, $\mathrm{Fin}$, $\mathrm{Inf}$, $\mathrm{Cof}$, $\mathrm{Rec}$, $\mathrm{Comp}$: sets of indices $e$ such that $\varphi_e$ is total, $W_e$ is finite, infinite, cofinite, decidable, Turing-equivalent to $K$ (classified examples).

Reductions, degrees, hierarchy

  • $A\le_mB$ (many-one), $A\le_1B$ (one-one), $A\le_TB$ (Turing); $\equiv_m$, $\equiv_1$, $\equiv_T$ the associated equivalences, $\deg_T(A)$ the Turing degree.
  • $A'=\{e:\varphi^A_e(e)\conv\}$ is the jump of $A$, $A^{(n)}$ the $n$-th jump; $\emptyset'$, the jump of the empty set, is $K$ itself (machines with oracle $\emptyset$ compute like machines without an oracle) (proposition).
  • $\Sigma_n$, $\Pi_n$, $\Delta_n$: classes of the arithmetical hierarchy (definition); $\Delta^1_1$: hyperarithmetical sets. Not to be confused with $\Sigma$ (an alphabet) or with $\Sigma(n)$ (busy beaver).

λ-calculus

  • $\mathrm{VL}(M)$: free variables; $M[x:=N]$: substitution; $=_\alpha$: α-equivalence; $\to_\beta$: one step of β-reduction, $\twoheadrightarrow_\beta$: its reflexive and transitive closure, $=_\beta$: β-equivalence (definition). Application associates to the left ($MNP=(MN)P$).
  • $\mathbf n=\lambda f.\lambda x.f^n x$ is the Church numeral (definition).
  • Simple types $A\to B$ (the arrow associates to the right), context $\Gamma$, typing judgement $\Gamma\vdash M:A$ (definition); $\mathbf N$, $\mathsf S$ and the recursor $\mathsf R$ of System T (definition). The same sign $\vdash$ is used for provability: this is the Curry-Howard correspondence.
  • System F: $\forall\alpha.A$ (polymorphic type), $\Lambda\alpha.M$ (type abstraction), $\mathrm{Nat}=\forall\alpha.(\alpha\to\alpha)\to\alpha\to\alpha$, whose inhabitants are still written $\mathbf n$ (definition). Do not confuse $\mathrm{Nat}$ (a type of F) with $\mathbf N$ (the base type of System T).
  • The λ-calculus and the halting set share two letters: $\mathsf K=\lambda x.\lambda y.x$ and $\mathsf S$ are combinators, $K$ is the halting set; $\Omega=(\lambda x.xx)(\lambda x.xx)$ is the term with no normal form, $\Omega$ (with no further qualification, from chapter 18 on) is Chaitin’s constant. The successor $\mathsf S$ of System T and the basic function $S$ of the recursive functions are the same idea.

Logic and incompleteness

  • $\mathsf{PA}$: Peano arithmetic; $\mathsf T\vdash\phi$: $\phi$ is provable in $T$; $\ulcorner\phi\urcorner$: Gödel number of the formula $\phi$; $\underline n$: the term $(\cdots(0+1)\cdots)+1$ representing $n$; $\mathrm{Prf}_{\mathsf T}$, $\mathrm{Prov}_{\mathsf T}$, $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf T)$: proof predicate, provability predicate and consistency statement, $\Box\phi$ abbreviating $\mathrm{Prov}_{\mathsf T}(\underline{\ulcorner\phi\urcorner})$ (definitions, derivability conditions).
  • Bounded formulas ($\Delta_0$) and $\Sigma_1$ formulas (definition).
  • $\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal M)$: set of sentences true in the structure $\mathcal M$; $\mathrm{Th}(\N,+)$: Presburger arithmetic (definition).

Kolmogorov complexity and Ω

  • $\{0,1\}^\ast$: binary words; $|w|$: length; $\varepsilon$: empty word; the integer $n$ is identified with the word obtained by writing $n+1$ in binary without its leading digit (length-lexicographic order); $\bar w=1^{|w|}0\,w$: self-delimiting code (convention).
  • $C(x)$: Kolmogorov complexity, $\mathit{KP}(x)$: prefix complexity, $\Omega$: halting probability of the universal prefix machine (definition, Ω).

Complexity

  • $\mathrm{DTIME}(t)$, $\mathrm{NTIME}(t)$, $\mathrm{DSPACE}(s)$, $\mathrm{NSPACE}(s)$; classes $\mathsf L$, $\mathsf{NL}$, $\mathsf P$, $\mathsf{NP}$, $\mathsf{PSPACE}$, $\mathsf{EXP}$; $\mathrm{co}\mathcal C$: complements (definition). $A\le_pB$: polynomial-time reduction (definition).
  • Probabilistic classes $\mathsf{RP}$, $\mathrm{co}\mathsf{RP}$, $\mathsf{BPP}$ (definition) and the quantum class $\mathsf{BQP}$ (definition); $|i\rangle$ (Dirac notation) denotes the basis state indexed by the word $i\in\{0,1\}^n$, and $\mathbb C$ the complex numbers.
  • $O$, $o$: Landau notation; $\log_2$: base-2 logarithm.

Diophantine equations

  • In $D(\vec a,\vec x)=0$, the $\vec a$ are the parameters and the $\vec x$ the unknowns; the unknowns range over $\N$ unless otherwise stated (definition).

Cellular automata

  • An elementary automaton has two states $0,1$ and the neighbourhood $(x_{i-1},x_i,x_{i+1})$; its Wolfram number $\sum f(a,b,c)\,2^{4a+2b+c}$ identifies it among the $256$ rules, hence “rule 110” (definition, Wolfram classes).

Words and vocabulary

  • The words “computable”, “recursive”, “Turing-computable”, “λ-definable” denote, once the equivalence of the models is established, the same class of functions; “decidable” and “recursive” are synonyms for sets, as are “semi-decidable” and “recursively enumerable”.
  • The letter $U$ has several uses, always specified: result function of the normal form, universal function, universal machine of Kolmogorov complexity, universal equation, black box $U_f$ and unitary operators of quantum computing.
